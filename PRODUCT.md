# Product brief — THE LLM

## Purpose

THE LLM: From Information to Generation is a Traditional Chinese, paper-first
learning site for graduate students who are new to language-model research. It turns
influential papers into readable explanations, then connects each paper to the
problem and decision that came before it.

## Audience

The primary reader is a university student who has just started graduate school,
regardless of department. They may know basic programming and linear algebra, but
they should not need a machine-learning vocabulary to begin.

## Promise

Every paper starts with a plain-language map, then slows down at the important
sentences, equations, assumptions, and trade-offs. The site distinguishes what the
paper actually showed from what later work made possible.

## Editorial position

The history is written as connected decisions rather than a parade of dates. Its
editorial spine is a respectful nod to James Gleick's The Information: a history,
a theory, and a flood. The site uses an information-dense, reported editorial voice
while keeping its own visual language and source links.

Each chapter has three turns:

1. **History** — the problem and research culture that made the paper necessary.
2. **Theory** — what the paper actually says, unpacked in plain language.
3. **Flood** — the later work, scale, and consequences that changed its meaning.

## Research standard

Long-form chapters are assembled from a versioned source ledger. A paper record must
include its DOI or an explicit DOI-not-found state, the author list, author briefs,
motivation claims, source IDs, confidence, and unknowns. Historical reconstruction
is allowed only when its evidence chain is visible; a compelling story is never a
license to invent a private conversation or a single-genius origin.

## First paper

The opening paper is Vaswani et al., “Attention Is All You Need” (2017). It is the
right first chapter because Transformer is the shared structural ancestor of most
modern LLM families, even though the paper itself is an encoder–decoder translation
system rather than a chat model.

## Content boundary

The site explains papers in original language and links to the authoritative paper.
It does not reproduce entire papers. Quotations remain short and are used only when
the exact wording is part of the lesson.
